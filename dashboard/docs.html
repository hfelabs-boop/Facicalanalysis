How each metric is calculated

Every number on the console comes from the steps below. Constants are the defaults in CogSenseConfig (cogsense/config.py). The browser/phone engine (dashboard/engine.js) is a port of the Python engine and gives the same results; a parity test checks this frame by frame.

Pipeline overview

  1. Landmarks. MediaPipe Face Landmarker returns 478 face-mesh points per frame, plus 52 learned blendshape scores.
  2. Normalize. The mesh is rotated into a face-anchored frame and scaled by inter-ocular distance, which removes head pose and camera distance.
  3. Features. 13 geometric measurements are taken from the normalized mesh (brow heights, eye openness, lip-corner depth, and so on).
  4. Baseline-relative evidence. Each feature is compared with the operator’s own neutral baseline and mapped to a 0–1 value per AU.
  5. Fusion & conditioning. Geometric evidence is blended with blendshape evidence; blinks and speech are masked; values are smoothed.
  6. Cognitive metrics. AUs, blink rate, gaze and posture feed MES, CFI and the event detectors.

Units & normalization

Landmarks arrive as (x/W, y/H, z/W). They are converted to pixels, then expressed in a face frame:

  • x axis runs from the subject’s right eye to the left eye (average of the outer corners, inner corners and the face sides).
  • y axis runs from forehead to chin, made perpendicular to x.
  • z axis is the cross product (pointing away from the camera).
  • Origin is the nose bridge (landmark 168).
  • Scale is the inter-ocular distance (IOD): the distance between the two eye centres.

All distances below are in IOD units. A brow shift of 0.08 means 8 % of the eye-to-eye distance, whatever the face size or camera distance. Head pose comes from the same frame:

yaw = atan2(R₀₂, R₂₂) pitch = asin(−R₁₂) roll = atan2(R₁₀, R₁₁)

Because every feature is measured in this frame, rotating the head within ±30° yaw, ±20° pitch or ±20° roll does not change the features (the tests check this).

Baseline calibration

AUs measure change from this person’s neutral face, not absolute shape. That absorbs resting brow height, natural asymmetry and habitual squint.

Action units

Each AU starts as a clipped 0–1 geometric evidence value. Let Δfeature = current − baseline, and clip(x) limit x to 0…1. The divisors are the scales in config: the displacement that counts as full intensity.

AU4 — Brow lowerer

Primary measure of mental workload and strain.

innerDrop = −(Δbrow_inner_h_right + Δbrow_inner_h_left) / 2 gapDrop = −Δbrow_gap AU4 = clip( 0.6 · innerDrop / 0.08 + 0.4 · gapDrop / 0.10 )

brow_inner_h is the height of the inner brow above the eye-corner line; brow_gap is the horizontal distance between the two inner brows. Lowering the brows and drawing them together both raise AU4.

AU1 — Inner brow raiser

AU1 = clip( −innerDrop / 0.08 ) (the inner brow rising above baseline)

AU2 — Outer brow raiser

outerRise = (Δbrow_outer_h_right + Δbrow_outer_h_left) / 2 AU2 = clip( outerRise / 0.09 )

AU1 and AU2 are reported separately; the automation-surprise detector uses their average.

AU7 — Lid tightener

Visual-search effort, fine discrimination, glare.

EAR = (|p2−p6| + |p3−p5|) / (2·|p1−p4|) eye aspect ratio, averaged over both eyes earDrop = 1 − EAR / EAR_baseline lidRise = −(Δlower_lid_right + Δlower_lid_left) / 2 lower lid moving up AU7 = clip( 0.5 · earDrop / 0.42 + 0.5 · lidRise / 0.05 )

While a blink is in progress the eye aperture collapses, so AU7 is held at its pre-blink value rather than reported as lid tightening.

AU14 — Dimpler

Frustration or annoyance (tightened lip corners).

AU14 = clip( 0.6 · Δlip_corner_depth / 0.06 + 0.4 · Δmouth_width / 0.06 )

lip_corner_depth is how far the mouth corners sit behind the centre of the lips. This is the weakest signal from a single camera, so it benefits most from blendshape fusion and from a trained model (see limits).

AU45 / AU43 — Blinks and eye closure

ratio = EAR / EAR_baseline closed when ratio < 0.45; reopens when ratio > 0.60 (hysteresis stops flicker)

Blendshape fusion

MediaPipe also outputs learned blendshape scores. Each is made baseline-relative, then averaged with the geometric evidence:

rel = clip( (score − resting) / (1 − resting) ) AU = 0.5 · geometric + 0.5 · blendshape

If a blendshape is missing for a frame, that AU uses geometry alone.

Smoothing

Each AU passes through an exponential moving average with a time constant of 0.04 s, adjusted for the actual time between frames:

α = 1 − exp(−Δt / 0.04) value ← value + α · (target − value)

This removes landmark jitter but still follows sub-500 ms transients.

Signal conditioning

Speech & chewing mask

Talking and chewing move the lower face rhythmically. The detector watches two signals over a 0.6 s window: inner-lip aperture and chin-to-nose distance.

std = max( stdev(aperture), stdev(jaw_drop) ) active = std > 0.018 OR ( mouth-open amount > 0.07 AND std > 0.009 ) stays active for 0.4 s after the last trigger

When active, speech_interference_detected is true, AU14 is multiplied by 0.3 and confidence by 0.85. Speech frames are also excluded from calibration.

Cognitive metrics

Mental Effort Score (MES, 0–100)

The PRD names the inputs (sustained AU4 and blink-rate suppression) but leaves the formula blank, so this is the implemented definition.

S = clip( 1 − blink_rate / resting_blink_rate ) blink-rate suppression e = 0.7 · AU4 + 0.3 · S per-frame effort MES = 100 · mean(e over the last 5 s)

If no blink rate is available yet (first 5 s), e = AU4. The resting blink rate comes from calibration (or 17/min).

Worked example. AU4 held at 0.60 for 5 s, blinking 8/min against a resting 17/min: S = 1 − 8/17 = 0.529, e = 0.7·0.60 + 0.3·0.529 = 0.579, so MES = 57.9. With no blink data the same face scores 60.0.

A person who stops blinking entirely adds up to 30 points even with a neutral brow, which is intended: suppressed blinking is a known sign of sustained visual attention.

Cognitive Friction Index (CFI, 0–1)

A continuous index that rises when a brow-lowering spike coincides with annoyance or erratic eye movement.

rise = AU4 − (lowest AU4 in the last 200 ms) C = max( clip(AU14 / 0.30), clip(saccade_rate / 3 Hz) ) co-occurrence CFI = clip( AU4 · (0.6 + 0.4 · C) + 0.15 · clip(rise / 0.25) ) CFI is halved if a task completion was logged in the last 2 s

So AU4 alone gives at most 0.6 of its value; AU14 or fast saccades lift that to the full AU4, and a sharp rise adds up to 0.15.

Worked example. Steady AU4 = 0.80, AU14 = 0.15 (C = 0.5), no recent rise: CFI = 0.80 · (0.6 + 0.4·0.5) = 0.64. If the operator had just completed a task (within 2 s): 0.32.

The console and report mark moments where CFI exceeds 0.70.

CFI event (discrete)

A CFI_EVENT is logged when all hold:

  1. AU4 spike: AU4 > 0.65 and it rose by ≥ 0.25 within 200 ms.
  2. Co-occurrence (within 300 ms of the spike): AU14 ≥ 0.30 or saccade rate ≥ 3 per second.
  3. No task completion in the previous 2 s (the effort was productive, not friction).
  4. At least 1 s since the previous CFI event.

The event records the peak CFI, which trigger fired (AU14 or SACCADES) and the active AOI.

Automation surprise

brow raise: (AU1 + AU2) / 2 > 0.50 surge: AU4 ≥ 0.50 and rose ≥ 0.20 within 200 ms event: a surge within 800 ms after a brow raise

It logs AUTOMATION_SURPRISE once and holds automation_surprise_flag for 1 s. The pattern is a startle or novelty response followed by effortful re-evaluation, which can mark a human-autonomy hand-off breakdown.

Fusion & insights

Gaze & AOI

  • Each face frame is matched to the nearest gaze sample within 250 ms; older gaze is ignored, so a stale AOI is never reported.
  • The active AOI is the rectangle containing the gaze point; if rectangles overlap, the one listed last (drawn on top) wins.
  • saccade_rate_hz counts samples flagged as saccades in the last 1 s.
  • RULA scores use the nearest sample within 2 s.

Compound posture risk

posture high = RULA neck ≥ 3 OR RULA trunk ≥ 3 risk = posture high AND ( AU7 ≥ 0.40 OR AU4 ≥ 0.50 )

A COMPOUND_POSTURE_RISK event is logged once when the condition starts, and again only after it has been clear for 1 s. A sustained condition therefore logs one event, not one per frame.

Correlated insight

One label per frame, chosen by the first rule that matches:

  1. FATIGUE_MICROSLEEP_RISK: eyes closed ≥ 500 ms
  2. AUTOMATION_SURPRISE: surprise flag is on
  3. POSTURE_DRIVEN_VISUAL_COMPENSATION: posture high and AU7 ≥ 0.40
  4. HIGH_COGNITIVE_STRAIN_ON_COMPLEX_WIDGET: CFI ≥ 0.70 while looking at an AOI
  5. HIGH_COGNITIVE_FRICTION: CFI ≥ 0.70, no AOI
  6. VISUAL_STRAIN_DISPLAY_LEGIBILITY: AU7 ≥ 0.40 with acceptable posture (suspect glare or low-contrast text, not posture)
  7. POSTURAL_LOAD_WITH_COGNITIVE_STRAIN: posture high and AU4 ≥ 0.50
  8. SUSTAINED_MENTAL_EFFORT: MES ≥ 60
  9. null: nothing notable

Timing & sync

  • Every frame is stamped at capture on one monotonic clock, so processing delay never becomes drift between streams.
  • UTC and LSL timestamps are derived by offsets that are re-checked every 10 s and slewed (at most 0.5 ms per check), never stepped, so time stays monotonic.
  • Each live payload carries clock_drift_ms, the current gap between derived UTC and the system clock, and processing_ms for the engine. Camera mode also reports inference_ms for the face tracker.
  • Inbound gaze, RULA and UI events may carry their own timestamp (t_utc_ms or t_lsl); they are matched by time, not arrival order.

What is and isn’t validated

  • Verified by tests: the maths above on synthetic faces (AU recovery across head pose, calibration, speech and blink handling, event timing) and that the browser engine matches the Python engine.
  • Not yet validated: accuracy against FACS-coded ground truth. The AU scales are hand-tuned. The PRD’s F1 ≥ 0.82 target on DISFA/BP4D is not yet met; the training tool (cogsense validate) exists, but those datasets are licensed and weren’t available.
  • MES and CFI weights (0.7/0.3, 0.6/0.4, 0.15) are design choices, not fitted to operator data. Treat absolute values as relative within a person and session until they are calibrated against ground truth such as NASA-TLX.
  • AU14 is weak from a monocular mesh; occlusion detection is heuristic (MediaPipe extrapolates hidden landmarks).

Tuning thresholds

Every constant on this page lives in CogSenseConfig. Override any of them with a JSON file:

{ "cfi_au4_threshold": 0.60, "mes_au4_weight": 0.8, "scales": { "brow_height": 0.07 } }

cogsense run --config overrides.json

The browser engine accepts the same keys: new CogSense.CogSenseEngine({ cfi_au4_threshold: 0.60 }).
